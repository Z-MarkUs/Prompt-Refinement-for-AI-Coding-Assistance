Paired comparison

p ≈ 0.34

No statistically demonstrated difference

For direct GPT-3.5 versus the fine-tuned-refiner route, 194 task IDs were paired: direct-only successes were 23 versus 16 refinement-only successes. The −3.61 percentage-point difference has an exact p-value of 0.3368.

Sensitivity check: excluding the three likely task-identity conflicts leaves 191 paired tasks, 22 direct-only versus 16 refinement-only successes, a −3.14 percentage-point difference, and p = 0.4177. The conclusion is unchanged.

Overlap risk check: excluding the two benchmark tasks with equivalent variants in the retained training export leaves 192 pairs. The 23 versus 16 discordances and exact p = 0.3368 are unchanged.

Read this correctly: this is evidence against claiming a gain—not proof that refinement can never help.

!
The negative result is the result.

This case study reports what happened without converting exploratory evidence into a success claim. That makes the work more useful: it exposes where the hypothesis needs better data, controls, and diagnosis.

05 · Boundaries

What this study can—and cannot—say.

Good evaluation makes the boundary visible. These results support a narrow claim about this historical benchmark, not a universal claim about prompt refinement.

Historical snapshot

Models, APIs, and judge behavior can change. This site reports archived study outputs and should not be read as current model performance.

Unequal published denominators

The original workflows contain 197, 196, and 199 recorded outcomes. The common 193-task view is the appropriate aligned descriptive comparison.

Correctness is not total quality

Judge acceptance tests functional correctness for the evaluated cases. It does not fully measure readability, maintainability, security, cost, or latency.

Causality remains unresolved

The observed outcomes do not isolate which prompt changes helped or harmed generation. A stronger follow-up would classify transformations and repeat trials.

Scope is coding tasks

The findings should not be generalized to writing, analysis, agentic workflows, or every kind of software-engineering assistance.

Legacy input quality

The benchmark audit flags repeated signatures and three likely prompt/signature conflicts. Fine-tuning checks find duplicate conversations, one additional repeated prompt, an empty assistant object, and an empty validation prompt. A hash-bound review confirms two equivalent task variants across the retained training export and benchmark, but the exact model/export binding is not recorded. Raw files remain unchanged; validation output and fingerprinted manifests make every decision explicit.

The next experiment

Turn the null result into a sharper question.

Future work can move from “does refinement help?” to “which transformations help which task types, under what cost and reliability constraints?”
